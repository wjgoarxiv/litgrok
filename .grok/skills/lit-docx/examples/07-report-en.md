# Summary

The team closed 18,420 tickets in the quarter, 14 % more than in the second quarter, while median first response time stayed at 2.1 hours. Satisfaction slipped from 4.4 to 4.2 out of 5, and almost all of the drop came from billing questions after the August price change.

::: keyfigures
- **18,420** tickets closed (sample)
  * July to September 2026, all channels
- **2.1 h** median first response (sample)
  * Business hours, email and web form
- **4.2 / 5** satisfaction score (sample)
  * Post-ticket survey, 3,960 responses
:::

Two changes would recover most of the lost satisfaction: a billing help page written with the finance team, and a second billing specialist on the late shift. Together they cost about £41,000 a year. We ask for approval of both before the November billing cycle.

# Volume and mix

## Tickets by channel

Email remained the largest channel, but chat grew fastest after we extended its hours to 20:00 in July. Phone volume fell for the third quarter in a row.

## Tickets by topic

Billing questions rose from 18 % to 29 % of all tickets. Most asked why an invoice had changed, how the new seat-based price was calculated, or how to move to annual payment. Login and access problems fell after the single sign-on fix shipped in July, and product how-to questions held steady.

# Speed of response

Median first response held at 2.1 hours across the quarter, inside the four-hour target. The tail is the concern. The slowest tenth of email tickets waited more than 11 hours for a first answer in August, compared with 7 hours in June, and nearly all of those were billing tickets that arrived after 17:00, when only one billing specialist is on shift.

Chat answered 91 % of conversations within two minutes. Resolution on first contact improved from 64 % to 68 %, helped by the new saved replies for login problems.

# Customer satisfaction

The survey score fell from 4.4 to 4.2. Splitting the responses by topic shows where the fall came from: billing tickets averaged 3.6, down from 4.3, while every other topic stayed within 0.1 of its second-quarter score. Comments on billing tickets mention the same few things again and again. Customers could not see how the new price was worked out, and the first answer often sent them to an invoice page that did not explain it either.

::: callout kind=note title="How the score is measured"
Customers receive a one-question survey when a ticket closes and answer on a scale of 1 to 5. The response rate this quarter was 21.5 %. Scores from fewer than 100 responses in a topic are not reported separately.
:::

# Staffing

The team had 38 full-time agents at the end of September, two more than in June. Attrition was one agent in the quarter. Average handling time rose from 9.4 to 10.8 minutes, mainly because billing tickets take longer to answer and needed checking against the finance system.

The late shift, 17:00 to 22:00, has six agents and one billing specialist. On the evenings after invoices go out, billing tickets on that shift reached 140, which one specialist cannot clear before morning.

# Proposals for the fourth quarter

## Billing help page

We propose a single help page that explains the seat-based price with three worked examples, written jointly with finance and linked from every invoice email. Similar pages for the login changes cut related tickets by about a third within six weeks. The cost is staff time only, about two weeks for one writer and one finance analyst.

## Second billing specialist on the late shift

We propose moving one agent into a billing specialist role on the late shift from 1 November, with a salary adjustment and two weeks of training with finance. The annual cost, including the adjustment and backfilling the agent's place, is about £41,000.

## Measures to watch

If both changes work, the 90th percentile first response for email should return below 8 hours in November and the billing satisfaction score should recover to at least 4.0 by the end of December. We will report both in the fourth-quarter review.
