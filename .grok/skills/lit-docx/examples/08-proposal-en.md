# Proposal in brief

The core facility runs nine shared instruments used by 214 researchers from 31 groups. Bookings live on paper sheets taped to the instrument doors and a spreadsheet that anyone can edit. Last year that arrangement produced 46 recorded double bookings, an estimated 610 hours of booked time that went unused, and a monthly charging exercise that takes the facility manager three working days.

::: keyfigures
- **46** double bookings recorded (sample)
  * Facility incident log, 2025–26 academic year
- **610 h** booked but unused instrument time (sample)

- **3 days** monthly effort to prepare charges (sample)

We propose a hosted booking system that shows live availability, lets only trained users book each instrument, releases slots that are not claimed within 15 minutes, and produces the monthly charges from the bookings themselves. The first year costs £38,500, including setup, and the following years about £14,000.

# The current arrangement

Each instrument has a paper sheet for the current week and a tab in a shared spreadsheet for later weeks. Users are meant to copy their booking from one to the other. In practice many do not, and the two records disagree several times a week. The facility manager settles disputes by email.

Training is tracked separately, in a list kept by the instrument specialists. Nothing stops an untrained user from booking a slot, and specialists report turning away an untrained user about twice a month after the slot has already blocked someone else.

Charging is the slowest part. At the end of each month the manager compares the spreadsheet with instrument log files, corrects the obvious errors, and sends each group leader a statement. Disputes about charges take another day or two to resolve.

# Functions of the system

## Booking and availability

Users would see each instrument's calendar on the web and on their phone, book in slots of 30 minutes, and receive a reminder the evening before. A slot not claimed by signing in at the instrument within 15 minutes would be released and offered to the waiting list. No-shows would be visible.

## Training and access

Only users whose training is recorded for an instrument could book it. Specialists would record training in the same system, so the list they keep today would no longer be needed. Users whose training lapses after a year without use would be asked to book a short refresher.

## Charging

Charges would be calculated from actual sign-in and sign-out times at the rate set for each instrument and user category. Group leaders would see their running total at any time, and the monthly statement would be produced automatically. Group leaders who dispute a charge would see the sign-in record behind it, which settles most questions about who used an instrument and for how long without an email exchange with the facility manager.

# Plan and timetable

Table 1. Twelve-month plan (sample)

| Phase | Months | Work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Procurement | November–December 2026 | Select supplier, agree contract |
| Setup | January–February 2027 | Configure instruments, rates and training records |
| Pilot | March–April 2027 | Two instruments, four research groups |
| Rollout | May–July 2027 | Remaining seven instruments, all groups |
| Review | October 2027 | Report to the committee |

The pilot uses the two busiest instruments, the 400 MHz NMR and the single-crystal diffractometer, because they account for most double bookings and would show quickly whether the release rule works.

From the second year the licence and support cost about £14,000 a year. The facility recovers part of its running costs through charges; recovering unused booked time would add an estimated £9,000 a year at current rates.

# Benefits and measures

The committee would judge the system on four measures at the October 2027 review: double bookings, booked but unused hours, the manager's time spent on charging, and the share of users who rate booking as easy in the annual facility survey. The targets are fewer than five double bookings a year, unused booked time below 200 hours, charging done in half a day, and at least 80 % rating booking as easy.

If the pilot shows that the release rule frustrates users more than it helps, the committee can lengthen the 15-minute window before rollout without changing the rest of the plan.
